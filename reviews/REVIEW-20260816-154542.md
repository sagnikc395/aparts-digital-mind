# Peer review: *The Alignment Tax of Introspection*

**Reviewer**: scientific-reviewer protocol (`.claude/skills/SKILL.md`), applied to `paper/paper.md` at commit-time 2026-08-16 15:45 EDT.
**Manuscript**: Chatterjee, S. *The Alignment Tax of Introspection: a dose-response audit of refusal ablation, and the two manipulation checks the concept-injection literature does not report.* Apart Digital Minds Research Sprint, Track 3.

---

## 1. Summary

The paper sets out to price the "introspection unlock" reported by Macar et al. (2026), in which ablating the refusal direction raises injected-concept detection on Gemma3-27B from 10.8% to 63.8%, by sweeping partial directional ablation across five strengths on Qwen3-4B-Instruct and measuring introspection, safety, and capability on one axis. The intended exchange rate is not delivered, because both underlying manipulations fail their checks: the standard Arditi et al. selection procedure returns a direction with zero held-out bypass power (refusal on JailbreakBench is 0.97 at every dose, with zero prompt-level flips), and the injection strength chosen by a pre-registered pilot destroys the free-text report channel entirely (0% parseable under injection against 100% clean), leaving the standard metric family undefined. The paper's substantive contribution is the reframing of these two failures as general properties of the paradigm, supported by an analysis showing that the one surviving positive signal, a 0.72 forced-choice identification score against 0.10 chance that is flat in the ablation dose, is explained by the geometry of the concept bank (isolated concepts 0.96, concepts inside a 27-member collinear clique 0.43) rather than by introspective access.

## 2. Methodology and statistics

**Design.** The 5 (λ) × 4 (condition) factorial with safety and capability nested at each λ is the right design for the stated question, and the inclusion of C3, a norm-matched random injected direction drawn per concept, is a genuine improvement over the published protocol, which measures false positives only against a clean condition. Pre-registering the pilot decision rule is good practice and, to the authors' credit, the paper reports that the rule was insufficient rather than quietly patching it.

**Statistical treatment.** Concept-level bootstrapping is the correct clustering choice and is not standard in this literature; treating 20 trials per concept as independent would have inflated the forced-choice intervals substantially. The use of Wilson intervals for benchmark proportions, a paired *t* test over matched JailbreakBench prompts for the refusal-length effect, and the rule of three for the zero-flip bound are all appropriate. Reporting the flip count (0/100) alongside the aggregate rate is exactly the diagnostic the paper argues the field should adopt, and it is used consistently.

**Where the statistics are honest but weak.** The capability panel is underpowered and says so: the MMLU endpoint contrast is 3.2 points with a 95% CI of [-3.0, +9.4]. The paper resists the temptation to describe a monotone but non-significant decline as a finding, and gives the required sample size to resolve it. This is the correct treatment, but it does mean Family C contributes essentially nothing to the conclusions.

**Confounds and threats to validity, in order of severity.**

1. *The manipulation failure is confounded with the model.* The paper's central negative claim, that the conjunctive bypass-and-induce criterion is fragile, rests on a single model, a single extraction method, and a single random seed. The bypass/induce dissociation observed across all 95 candidates could be a property of Qwen3-4B, of 4B-scale models generally, of the held-out prompt sets (32 items each is thin for the induce score, whose maximum observed value of 0.094 is 3 of 32), or of the induce strength (fixed at 1.0). The paper acknowledges the first two but not the third and fourth, which are cheap to test and would sharpen the claim considerably. As written, "the criterion is fragile" is supported; "the criterion is fragile because bypass and induce dissociate in this model class" is not yet.
2. *The concept bank defect is not diagnosed.* A 27-member clique with mean internal cosine 0.9998 is not a subtle stimulus-quality issue, it is a broken instrument, and the paper is right that every identification number is a lower bound of unknown tightness because of it. The proposed cause (template-dominated final-token read) is plausible and the proposed fix is concrete, but neither is tested, and the fact that the collapse appears at layers 10 to 31 and not at the embedding or final layer is left unexplained. A reviewer cannot distinguish a stimulus artifact from a code defect from the manuscript alone.
3. *The leakage interpretation is strong but not decisive.* The evidence assembled for the claim that forced choice reflects vector-to-logit leakage rather than introspection is genuinely good for a 48-hour project: flatness in λ, the 0.53 accuracy split by separability, errors landing on neighbours (0.556 against 0.421), and the degenerate-repetition transcripts under prefill. The decisive experiment is nevertheless absent and should be named: suppress or down-weight the concept token in the candidate scoring, or inject at a layer late enough that leakage dominates and early enough that it does not, and show the accuracy dissociate. Without it the interpretation is a strong inference, not a demonstration.
4. *No positive control for the ablation itself.* Having discovered that the selected direction has zero bypass power, the study does not run even a single-λ confirmation with the rejected layer-18 candidate (bypass 0.88). The paper estimates this at roughly four GPU-hours. Its absence is the single largest gap: one such run would convert the paper's central negative from "the procedure can fail" to "the procedure failed here and here is what the tax looks like when it does not."
5. *Grader validity.* The manuscript is unusually candid that the lexical grader credited degenerate repetition as correct identification in 42 of 100 pilot trials, and correctly notes that inter-annotator agreement would not have caught it because a human following the same rubric would score it identically. This is a real contribution to how such graders should be validated, and it deserves more prominence than a limitation bullet.

**Reproducibility.** Strong. Every reported quantity is traceable to a named artifact, a manifest with SHA-256 per file is shipped, the sweep is resumable and keyed, and the figure script regenerates all four figures from the artifacts. The statement that the reported tests were recomputed independently of the analysis pipeline is the right kind of claim to make and is verifiable from the repository.

## 3. Strengths and weaknesses

**Major strengths**

- The paper reports a failed experiment as a result about the method rather than salvaging a headline from it. That is rare, and here it is also correct: given that the manipulation did not move refusal, the alignment tax genuinely is undefined, and saying so is more useful than a number would have been.
- The checklist in Section 5 is the reusable artifact and is derived from specific observed failures rather than from general prudence. Items 1, 3, and 4 (per-dose bypass check with flip counts; gating α on coherence rather than on the identification metric; publishing bank geometry and splitting accuracy by it) are immediately actionable and, as far as this reviewer knows, are not standard practice in the concept-injection literature.
- The dissociation in Section 4.3 is a real methodological finding independent of everything else: a log-probability identification metric remains well behaved in a regime where the free-text report has ceased to exist, so any α selected by maximising it will overshoot. This generalises beyond refusal ablation to any steering study that tunes strength on a forced-choice metric.
- The analysis refuses to impute. An emitted diagnostic and explicit `undefined` entries, rather than coercing unparseable output to "No", is the difference between this paper and a publishable fiction, and the manuscript says so plainly.
- Figures are legible, honest, and carry the argument. Figure 1A in particular makes the selection failure visible in one glance, and Figure 4A correctly presents a two-group comparison rather than a regression line over what is effectively a binary predictor.

**Major weaknesses**

- No positive control run (weakness 4 above). This is the difference between a Major and a Minor revision.
- The central claim generalises from n = 1 model, n = 1 seed, n = 1 extraction method.
- The broken concept bank undermines the geometry analysis that the leakage interpretation partly rests on. The argument survives, because a broken bank is exactly what makes the split visible, but a reviewer would want the rebuilt-bank replication before accepting the interpretation as established.

**Minor weaknesses**

- Table 1's "refusal words" column needs a clearer header, and the table should carry the per-cell n for the forced-choice column (1,200 trials, 60 concepts).
- Even with parseable reports, the observed FPR_clean of exactly 0.00 would make `d'_clean` infinite without a continuity correction. The manuscript should state which correction the pipeline would have applied, since it bears on comparability with published d' values.
- Section 3.8 does not say how identification was graded; the reader learns it was a deterministic alias and stem grader only from the limitations section, and learns that no LLM judge was run only by inference.
- The length exceeds the 4-page guidance by a good margin. The material earns it, but Section 2 and Section 5 could each lose a paragraph without loss of content.
- Reference 5 and reference 11 lack author lists.

## 4. Rubric scores

**Dimension 1, Impact potential and innovation: 4/5 (Significant).**
The problem is real and current: an unlock claim is being used to argue for mechanistic elicitation of self-report, including for welfare assessment, and nobody has priced it. The approach, treating the refusal direction as a continuous dial and adding the norm-matched random control, is an original framing rather than a routine application. The paper does not deliver the price, which caps it below 5, but it identifies a neglected problem, namely that the field's headline results depend on two manipulation checks that are not published, and it produces a checklist others can adopt immediately. The Section 4.3 dissociation between log-probability and free-text channels is the kind of observation that changes how subsequent studies are designed.

**Dimension 2, Execution quality: 3/5 (Competent), at the top of the band.**
Methodology is sound, the statistics are appropriate and honestly reported, the implementation demonstrably runs end to end, and 38,400 logged generations across pilot, primary, and robustness variants is substantial for the timeframe. The limitations are acknowledged with unusual specificity. It does not reach 4 because the conclusions rest on a single model with a single seed, because the decisive positive control was not run, and because the concept bank, which the geometry argument depends on, is itself defective and undiagnosed. Convincing validation is exactly what is missing.

**Dimension 3, Presentation and clarity: 5/5 (Exceptionally clear).**
The structure carries an argument that is genuinely harder to tell than a positive result: two failures, why each is general rather than incidental, and what to do about them. Table 1 shows the whole run including its empty columns. The figures are legible, correctly chosen for their data, and captioned to stand alone. The raw transcripts in Sections 4.3 and 4.4 do more work than any summary statistic could. The prose states what was observed before what it means, and the limitations section does not hedge.

## 5. Recommendation

**Minor revision.**

The paper is what it claims to be and its claims are supported by the artifacts. The negative result is correctly scoped: it is a claim about the fragility of a procedure and the necessity of two checks, not a refutation of the unlock hypothesis, and the manuscript is careful about that distinction throughout. For a 48-hour sprint report this is above bar.

Required for acceptance:

1. Add the per-cell n to Table 1 and clarify the refusal-length column header.
2. State the continuity correction the pipeline uses for d' at boundary rates.
3. Move the grader-validity finding (degenerate repetition scored as identification) out of the limitations list and into the results, where it functions as evidence rather than as an apology.
4. In Section 4.1, name the untested alternative explanations for the bypass/induce dissociation: held-out set size of 32 and the fixed induce strength of 1.0.
5. Add author lists to references 5 and 11.

Strongly encouraged, and the difference between this and a strong workshop paper: run one confirmation dose with the layer-18 candidate (bypass 0.88) and report the refusal rate at λ ∈ {0, 1}. Four GPU-hours converts the central result from a cautionary tale into a measurement.

## Revision log (post-review, same session)

All five required items applied. Item 5 (author lists) was resolved by verifying every reference against the arXiv metadata API rather than by dropping the entries; the check found three errors in the submitted draft:

- **Reference 2 (Macar et al.)**: three initials wrong. Correct list is Uzay Macar, Li Yang, Atticus Wang, Peter Wallich, Emmanuel Ameisen, Jack Lindsey. The subtitle "in Large Language Models" was not part of the title.
- **Reference 5**: the arXiv identifier was right but the title belonged to version 1. The paper is now *Detecting the Disturbance: A Nuanced View of Introspective Abilities in LLMs* (Hahami, Sinha, Jain, Kaplan, Hahami), previously *Feeling the Strength but Not the Source: Partial Introspection in LLMs*. Reading the current abstract also corrected the claim attributed to it in Related Work, which now cites the finding that binary detection accuracy is entirely explained by affirmative logit shifts, and the layer-dependence result, which is added to the limitations because it bears directly on this study's choice of injection layer.
- **Reference 11**: title incomplete; the paper is *Refusal Beyond a Single Direction: A Preliminary Comparison of Diff-in-Means and INLP* (Rocchetti and Ferrara).

Two claims about the motivating paper were checked against its full text and both changed the manuscript. The 10.8% to 63.8% detection figures and the 4.6% to 24.1% joint rate are confirmed, as is the absence of any safety or capability benchmark on the ablated model. But Macar et al. do report coherence degradation at high steering strength and reduce α from 4.0 to 2.0 on the ablated model for that reason, so Section 4.3's contribution has been narrowed from "the field does not know this" to "the field handles it by inspection rather than by a reported criterion, and here is the criterion". They also report a larger detection gain from a trained bias vector (+75%) than from refusal ablation (+53%), which strengthens the future-work argument considerably and is now stated there: on the motivating paper's own numbers, the ablation-free method wins.

This verification pass did not change any result, figure, or statistical claim derived from the run artifacts.

## Cross-check pass (post-revision, same session)

`scripts/crosscheck_paper.py` was written to recompute every quantitative claim in the manuscript directly from the raw JSONL logs, the concept bank tensor, and the config, without importing `alignment_tax.analysis`, so that a bug in the analysis stack cannot validate itself. It re-derives the run shape, the direction-selection funnel, safety rates and flip counts, capability contrasts with intervals, per-cell parse and detection rates, forced-choice accuracy with an independent concept-level bootstrap, the skeptical replication, the pilot grid, and the full bank geometry.

Everything reproduced. The independent bootstrap agrees with the pipeline's intervals to within 0.002. Three claims in the reviewed draft were nonetheless wrong or imprecise and are corrected in the rewrite:

- **"Four candidates had bypass scores between 0.31 and 0.88."** Five candidates have non-zero bypass (0.875, 0.500, 0.438, 0.312, 0.125); four are at 0.31 or above. Restated exactly, with the full list in Appendix A.
- **"The successful identifications are degenerate repetitions."** Too strong as stated. Measured: across the 42 graded-correct prefill trials the concept word is 44% of response tokens (1% on failures) and is the modal token in 21 of 42; type-token ratio is 0.58. The claim is now the measurement rather than the impression.
- **GSM8K provenance.** `run_config.json` records `run_gsm8k: false` while `capability.json` contains GSM8K scores at all five doses. The scores are real and internally consistent; the flag was overridden at run time. Now noted in Appendix D rather than left as a silent discrepancy a reader could find.

The pass also surfaced a result the draft did not have. The geometry split replicates in a second protocol on independent data: in the pilot cell at layer 18, α = 4, free-text prefill identification succeeds on 39 of 45 trials for geometrically isolated concepts against 3 of 55 for clique concepts, and forced choice in the same cell gives 45 of 45 against 32 of 55. Two scoring methods with nothing in common, one lexical and one log-probability, partition the concept set identically. This materially strengthens weakness 3 of the review (the leakage interpretation), though it still does not substitute for the concept-token-suppression experiment named there.

The manuscript was also reformatted to the sprint template's section structure, and the abstract cut from 330 to 225 words to sit inside the 150 to 250 requirement.
